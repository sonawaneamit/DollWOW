# Replacement Parts: The Model Details You Need to Keep

The most useful replacement-parts information is often easiest to collect before anything needs replacing. Save the body and head codes, material, colour identifiers, connector details and documentation while the order is still fresh. A marketing name alone may not identify the component you need a year later.

A good record helps support locate the right information. It does not guarantee that a part is available or that a repair can be completed at home.

## Begin with the body and head as separate components

Keep the manufacturer's full body identifier and head identifier, not just the store's product name. Include the materials of both parts, the original tone selection and any documented production version.

For example, "replacement controller" is not a complete part description. Record the controller's model label and the powered system it operates, then ask whether the proposed replacement is approved for that system. This is an identification example, not a claim that a particular controller is stocked.

Also keep the final configuration you bought. A later visit to a product page may show updated options or a different standard build. Your order record is the starting point for identifying the existing doll, not proof that every component is still sold.

## Photograph labels while they are accessible

Take clear photographs of external component labels, connectors and supplied accessories. Include an overall view so a close-up can be connected to the correct system. Photograph the packaging label of a spare part before discarding it.

For a connector question, ask support which views or measurements are needed. Avoid dismantling a joint or opening the body just to complete the record. A ruler beside an accessible component may help communicate scale, but a photograph is not a substitute for a specified connector drawing.

Keep the original files rather than only compressed chat copies. If you share pictures for support, leave personal addresses and unrelated order information out of the frame.

## Separate replaceable accessories from internal repairs

A wig, external controller and internal skeleton component represent very different support questions. Do not assume that because one part is removable, another part from the same brand is a routine home replacement.

Record what support has actually confirmed: the component identifier, which build it fits and whether installation needs an approved procedure or specialist work. A head that mechanically attaches may still have an unsuitable weight or visible colour difference.

For powered systems, the exact supply and controller matter alongside the connector. Matching socket shapes are not enough to approve a substitution. Leave an uncertain system unpowered while the replacement is checked.

## Keep a short change history

When an approved part is replaced, add the new identifier and date without deleting the original. Note whether an adapter or updated controller was supplied. This gives the next support conversation a clear history rather than two conflicting photographs.

Do the same for repairs or finish changes that could affect matching. If the body colour has been modified, the original tone name no longer tells the whole visual story.

This record complements the [maintenance checklist](/learn/sex-doll-maintenance-checklist). One concerns what to inspect; the other preserves the identity needed when an inspection raises a question.

## Send a focused request when you need a part

Start with the product identity, the affected component, what changed and clear photographs. Explain whether the part is missing, damaged or simply being considered as an alternative. Do not keep testing a damaged powered component to produce a longer description.

Review [Care for Life](/care-for-life) for DollWOW's support arrangements, then contact [support](/support) with the relevant record. Ask about current availability, fit, installation and any charge separately. A complete record makes that conversation more precise without promising stock, universal interchangeability or a free replacement.
