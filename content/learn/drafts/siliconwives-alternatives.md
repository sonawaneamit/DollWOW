## TPE, Silicone, and Hybrid Need Separate Comparisons

The name Silicon Wives can make material especially prominent in the buyer’s mind, but a store name does not identify the construction of every product. Read the exact listing.

### Full TPE

Full-TPE dolls are often considered for softness and value. Formulation, firmness, surface behavior, weight, and care can vary by brand and body. Compare listed weight, measurements, storage needs, and product-specific care.

### Full silicone

Full-silicone dolls can support detailed sculpting and a controlled surface finish. Silicone formulas and body designs still vary in firmness, stretch, weight, and maintenance. Material alone does not prove realism, durability, or easy handling.

### Hybrid construction

A hybrid may combine a silicone head with a TPE body or use another mixed construction. It can balance different appearance, feel, care, and price priorities. It should not be compared with a full-silicone doll as though the material and cost structure were identical.

## Product Images and Review Photos

Images should help identify the product, but they need context. Ask whether an image is:

- manufacturer reference photography
- an exact ready-stock unit
- custom factory media
- customer-submitted review media
- a clearly labeled promotional enhancement based on the product

Manufacturer images may show optional styling, clothing, wigs, or accessories. Review photos may show a custom configuration that differs from the current base model. Factory media can verify visible build details but may not prove internal construction or long-term performance.

Use several angles and close-ups. Compare face, hands, feet, skin finish, proportions, and the head-to-body pairing. Confirm what is included before treating the image as the final order.

## Ready-to-Ship and Made-to-Order

Ready-to-ship can suit a buyer who accepts a confirmed existing configuration and values a shorter timeline. Made-to-order can suit a buyer who needs specific supported options and accepts production time.

For ready-to-ship, verify the exact unit, fixed configuration, warehouse, current stock, dispatch expectation, and whether the photos show that unit. For made-to-order, verify option compatibility, production timing, approval steps, and when changes become final.

## Common Comparison Mistakes

### Treating a store rating as product proof

A store rating can help assess general experience. It does not confirm the material, measurements, configuration, or availability of the doll you want.

### Applying one review to an entire brand

Brands can offer many bodies, materials, heads, and options. A review of one model should not be treated as a test of every product.

### Comparing TPE and silicone by reputation alone

Both materials vary. Compare the exact construction, weight, finish, care routine, and price.

### Assuming every custom option is compatible

An option list is not proof that every combination can be produced. Confirm the complete build before production.

### Comparing discounts instead of configured totals

Match the product, options, shipping, destination, and availability before judging price.

### Waiting until after payment to ask about privacy or timing

Ask before checkout. DollWow can confirm the expected route, communication, and current product status while your choices remain flexible.

### Treating delivery as the end of the relationship

Arrival inspection, care, minor damage, replacement parts, and repair coordination can matter long after the original comparison.
