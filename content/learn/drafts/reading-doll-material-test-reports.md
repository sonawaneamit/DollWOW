# How to Read a Doll Material Test Report: A Buyer's Worksheet

A useful material test report answers a narrow question about an identified sample under stated conditions. It does not automatically establish the performance of every doll from a brand. Start with the sample description, the test performed and the connection to the product you are considering.

You do not need to become a laboratory specialist to spot a missing page or an unsupported leap from "sample tested" to "everything certified." The worksheet below helps you ask more precise questions. It does not review an actual supplier report or establish a result for any DollWOW product.

## First identify which document you have

A laboratory test report, a supplier declaration and a seller authorization certificate serve different purposes. A seller authorization concerns a commercial relationship. It is not a material test.

The [DollWOW authorized-vendors page](/authorized-vendors) is the place to review seller authorization information. Use a separate record for material evidence so that one document is not asked to prove something outside its purpose.

Likewise, a laboratory's accreditation is not a blanket approval of products tested there. [ILAC explains](https://ilac.org/about-ilac/faqs/) that laboratories undertake testing within their accredited scope and that accreditation bodies maintain their lists of accredited facilities. ILAC does not itself issue product test reports.

## Check identity before reading the result

Look for the report number, issuing laboratory, issue date, page numbering and sample description. Check whether the file is the complete report rather than a cropped result table or a promotional cover sheet.

Then connect the sample to the purchase. Does it name a material formulation, colour, component or finished product? Does the supplier explain which current body or head uses that material? A sample labelled only "silicone" leaves a significant identification question unanswered.

The date matters because the material or production specification may have changed. Age alone does not make a report invalid, but a seller should be able to explain why it still applies to the current build.

## A hypothetical report-reading worksheet

This is a hypothetical exercise, not a supplier report, a case study or a finding about a particular doll. Imagine receiving a document that identifies a coloured material sample and lists results for selected substances.

| Field to inspect | Useful interpretation | Question left open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sample description | Identifies what was submitted | Is this the current head or body material? |
| Test method and version | Defines the procedure used | Does that procedure address your question? |
| Listed substances | Shows the scope of this test | What was not tested? |
| Result and units | Reports the measured outcome | What do the units and reporting limits mean? |
| Pass statement | Compares results with stated criteria | Which criteria and decision rule were used? |

If the sample is a material piece, do not silently relabel it a finished-doll test. Paint, adhesive, hair attachments, electronics and other components may sit outside that sample's scope.

For a more concrete exercise, suppose the fictional sample sheet names a blue silicone material swatch, Sample H, and records Substance A as "less than 0.1 mg/kg." The names and numbers are invented to illustrate reading, not actual material results or safety limits.

| Fictional sheet detail | Warranted reading | Unsupported shortcut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sample H: blue silicone swatch | This result concerns the identified sample | Every head, body and paint from the brand was tested |
| Substance A: less than 0.1 mg/kg | Result is below the stated reporting value for this test | The substance is entirely absent |
| No specification or decision rule supplied | Ask what criteria would support a pass statement | Declare the finished doll certified or legally approved |
| No link to current product formulation | Request that link before applying the result to a purchase | Assume a similar material name proves it applies |

Your summary might be: "This document reports the listed tests on Sample H; the connection to the current body still needs confirmation." That is more informative than either dismissing the report or calling the whole product certified. The example demonstrates how to limit a claim, not how to assess toxicological risk.

## Read the limits as carefully as the headline

"Not detected" should be read with the method and reporting limit, not as a promise that no amount exists. A result expressed in one unit should not be compared with a limit expressed in another without a correct conversion and an understanding of the measurement basis.

If a conformity statement uses "pass," look for the specification it refers to. [ILAC's guidance directory](https://ilac.org/publications-and-resources/ilac-guidance-series/) includes guidance on decision rules, measurement uncertainty and accreditation scopes. These are reasons to ask the laboratory or an appropriately qualified reviewer for interpretation when a technical conclusion affects your purchase.

Do not turn a narrow chemical test into a claim about long-term durability, stain resistance, electrical safety or health benefits. Those are different questions requiring their own evidence.

## Verify the issuer independently

Use the laboratory's independently located contact information or verification service to check the report number. For accreditation claims, check the relevant accreditation body's directory and the laboratory's applicable scope. ILAC provides a [signatory directory](https://ilac.org/signatory-search/) to help locate those bodies.

Keep the original file and your questions together. If the seller can provide only an unreadable image or cannot connect the sample to the current material, treat that as unresolved evidence rather than guessing a reassuring answer.

## Ask for the claim you actually need supported

Send [DollWOW support](/support) the product link, the report and your specific question. "Does this report cover the selected body's material?" is easier to resolve than "Is everything safe?"

A good buying decision uses the report within its limits, alongside clear product identity, care instructions and seller support. No single badge can replace that combination.
