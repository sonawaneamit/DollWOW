# Can You Change the Head Later? A Compatibility Worksheet

Sometimes, yes. A replacement head needs to match more than the visible connector: the neck fit, supported weight, material, finish and any functions must work with your exact body. A similar-looking screw or a shared brand name is not enough to approve a pairing.

The easiest time to plan a second head is before buying the first build. You can ask for a supported pairing while the complete specification is in front of you.

## Begin with the body you actually own

Find the order record and copy the manufacturer, full body code, material and production or purchase date. Add the original head code and any connector description supplied with the order.

If you bought a warehouse unit, use that unit's specification rather than a current custom listing with a similar name. A later product revision may have a different neck arrangement. Photographs of the existing connection can help support identify it, but do not dismantle concealed parts just to obtain a picture.

The [custom doll guide](/learn/custom-sex-dolls) covers the broader build decision. For a replacement, keep attention on the existing body and the proposed head as one pairing.

## Compare connector types before comparing brands

We separate the connector label from a confirmed head-and-body pairing. A factory statement about a thread is useful evidence, but it does not settle the neck opening, seating depth, supported weight, wiring or finish. Our lookup below records the statement and its limits rather than turning a shared number into a fit guarantee.

### M16 threaded connections

A description of an M16 threaded connector is a starting point. Obtain the thread pitch, usable engagement, which mating part is supplied and the exact head/body versions before ordering. Do not silently expand an M16-only statement into a verified M16x2.0 specification.

Dolls Castle told us on 24 October 2026 that its heads use an M16 threaded connector and generally fit bodies using that connector. We have not independently tested a cross-brand pair. Pitch, model-specific neck seating and any material-specific exceptions remain unconfirmed.

SY Doll told us on 23 October 2026 that all its dolls use M16 screws. We record that statement for SY/Moonvale, but its broad compatibility claim is not a test of your proposed pair. The reply did not separately specify pitch, quick-connect construction, movable-jaw exceptions or adapters.

### Quick-connect and head-fixed arrangements

A quick-release fitting and a threaded fitting should not be treated as the same complete interface because their descriptions share a nominal size. Ask which part stays in the head, which part stays on the body, how the lock engages and whether both mating pieces are supplied. A photographed connector without dimensions is not enough to approve a replacement.

We do not have an exact-pair confirmation here for an M16 quick-connect or ball-lock conversion. Do not buy a conversion based on another model's photograph.

### Smaller screws and proposed adapters

Gynoid reported M8 in its 24 October reply. On 27 October 2026 it clarified that M8 applies to all dolls currently on sale and that it offers no adapter. The clarification did not separately break down TPE and silicone models. This is a manufacturer statement, not a measured thread specification or proof of cross-brand fit.

An M8-to-M16 or M12-to-M16 product name does not establish that it fits a particular head and body. We have not verified an adapter for Gynoid or resolved the conflicting M8/M12 descriptions for Zelex. Do not substitute one size for another or treat a FanReal series reference as universal support.

### Older or proprietary connections

We have not verified the proposed M14/older Sino and pre-2019 Irontech specifications for an exact version. EX Doll and Doll Sweet connector details also remain unconfirmed in this worksheet. Keep the production date, original fitting and model code attached to the question; do not assign a connector from brand name alone.

## A factory example, not a universal standard

Climax's official ULW 885 Renata collection identifies a standard M16 neck screw for its head option. That is useful information about that product family, not a guarantee that any head advertised as M16 will fit it. [Climax's ULW 885 specification](https://www.climax-doll.com/collections/ulw-885-torso-sex-doll)

Irontech's FAQ also describes arranging a different head and body combination, while separately directing buyers to option-compatibility guidance. That supports asking about a pairing; it does not establish an unrestricted cross-brand replacement system. [Irontech's factory FAQ](https://www.irontechdoll.com/help-faq/)

Keep confirmed combinations separate from proposed ones. A missing factory answer should remain "not yet confirmed," rather than becoming a recommendation through repetition.

For the Renata example, the supported statement is limited: the factory offers a head selection on ULW 885 and identifies M16 hardware. That does not confirm a separately purchased replacement head's code, seating dimensions or colour match. This worksheet contains no independently verified cross-brand pairing.

## Complete this worksheet before ordering

| Check | What you need confirmed |
| --- | --- |
| Exact identities | Existing body code and proposed head code, including versions |
| Connection | Thread or quick connector, mating parts, dimensions and any adapter |
| Neck fit | Neck opening, seating depth, clearance and movement |
| Head weight | Whether the body and neck arrangement support the selected head |
| Materials | Head and body materials, with care instructions for each |
| Appearance | Tone names, finish and likely visible junction |
| Functions | Required wiring, power or other connections and supported combinations |
| Supply | Head alone or head with the required connector parts, wig and accessories |

Ask for confirmation of the complete pairing in writing. A statement that the head "fits many dolls" leaves too much open.

## Work through a hypothetical request

Suppose you own Body A and want Head B. These are placeholders, not tested models. Your order record gives Body A's code and material, while Head B's listing gives only a connector label and photographs.

At that point, the connection is a lead to investigate, not a pass. Send both links and ask the supplier to confirm mating hardware, neck seating, head weight and tone. If the connector is confirmed but the neck clearance is not, the overall pairing is still unresolved.

If the mechanical fit is approved but the finish differs, you have a different decision: whether you accept the visible mismatch. Record that tradeoff before ordering. This keeps appearance preferences from being mistaken for a mechanical fault later.

| Reply to that hypothetical request | Worksheet status | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Only the connector label matches | Mechanical fit unconfirmed | Request neck seating, mating parts and supported head weight |
| Hardware fits; required function is not supported | Not suitable for that intended function | Choose a supported pairing or revise the requirement |
| Mechanical fit confirmed; tone differs | Appearance decision remains | Review a relevant reference and record whether the difference is acceptable |
| Exact pairing and supplied parts confirmed | Ready to review installation instructions | Keep the dated confirmation with both codes; do not generalize it to other heads |

These are decision states, not tested combinations. A confirmation for Body A and Head B does not clear Body A with another head that uses the same connector label.

## Skin-tone names are not colour standards

Two manufacturers can use the same tone name for different colours. Lighting, camera processing and makeup also affect photographs. Ask for a relevant reference rather than matching two unrelated promotional galleries by eye.

For an older body, explain its age and finish when asking about the replacement. A newly supplied head may not reproduce the appearance of a body that has already been handled and cleaned over time. Do not request a promise that photographs cannot support.

## Installation is a separate question

Once compatibility is confirmed, obtain the installation instructions for that connection. Do not force a part that resists, use the head as a lifting handle, or improvise an adapter from a similar-looking fitting.

An adapter can change the junction as well as the mating hardware. Ask for the assembled neck height, clearance, supported load and any function limitations for the exact pair. A mechanically attached head can still sit too high, conceal the wrong area or leave a visible gap. Agree on those tradeoffs before paying for the replacement.

Do not apply threadlocker, glue, a lubricant or a silicone/TPE contact barrier based on generic forum advice. Ask the manufacturer for the exact permitted product and method for both materials and the fitting. We have not established one chemical treatment or contact-barrier recipe that is safe for every build.

Plan supported storage for a spare head and clarify whether its wig is included. Keep the new head's code with the original order record, so future care questions identify which head is attached.
