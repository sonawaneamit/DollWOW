# Doll Body Painting: A Photo Inspection Checklist

A body-painting upgrade is easier to judge when you know what changed between the photographs. If the body, skin tone, lighting and finish all differ, an attractive comparison can still tell you very little about the option you are paying for.

Start with the exact manufacturer's option name and the actual body. "Realistic," "premium" and "standard" do not define one shared finish across every doll brand.

## Keep four different choices separate

| Choice | What to identify |
| --- | --- |
| Material and construction | What the body is made from |
| Skin tone | The selected base colour |
| Surface texture | Which documented surface detail applies |
| Body painting or makeup | Which additional visible finish is included or selected |

One label does not establish the others. A more detailed painted finish is not proof of a different material. A texture option is not automatically a body-painting option. A warm photograph is not proof that a warmer skin tone was supplied.

## Ask a fair question of the photos

The most useful comparison identifies the same body and tone with the two finishes named. Similar framing and lighting make it easier to see which details actually differ.

Use this checklist to inspect a reference and identify a useful follow-up view, not to rank two tested finishes. The cover is an editorial illustration, not a paint sample. Use a factory reference for the exact body and named option when making a purchase decision.

If the examples show different builds, treat them as illustrations rather than a controlled test. The same caution applies when comparing a polished campaign image with a quick factory picture. Background colour, light direction and exposure can all affect the impression.

Use the complete gallery to understand the look. Do not assume that the most dramatic single close-up represents the entire body under every light.

## Turn an impression into a specific photo request

| Inspection question | Useful view or record | Limit to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is the finish named? | Exact body, base tone and factory option label beside the reference | An attractive image alone does not identify the option |
| Is detail visible over the whole body? | Wider view plus close views of the areas that matter to you | One detailed patch does not establish uniform detail |
| Is a tone transition visible? | Head/neck and adjacent body in the same frame | Separate head and body shots cannot establish a colour match |
| Could lighting explain the sheen? | Another angle or ordinary-light view of the same finish | A bright highlight is not a texture measurement |
| Does the reference match the order? | Image reference linked to the written selected finish | Photos do not establish whether an upgrade is included in the price |

A useful hypothetical request is: "The close-up shows the shoulder finish, but I cannot see how it meets the neck. Is a same-frame view available for this body, tone and option?" It identifies the missing evidence without inventing a defect or demanding an exact screen-colour match.

## Match the finish to the order summary

Check whether the pictured finish is standard for the exact model, a separately selected upgrade or part of a current offer. A brand-level banner is not a reliable list of everything included in every SKU.

When comparing two prices, set both builds to the finish you actually want. Otherwise you may be comparing an upgraded photograph against a base-price configuration.

Keep head makeup separate from body painting as well. A face can be shown with a distinctive makeup style while the body is supplied under a different option description. The written head and body choices should make that distinction clear.

## A hand-finished product is not a printed image

Irontech's FAQ notes that its painted reference effects can vary slightly and that friction and cleaning can gradually affect body painting. It recommends gentle dressing and smooth fabrics to reduce friction. Those are manufacturer-specific care statements, not a universal lifespan prediction. [Irontech care guidance](https://www.irontechdoll.com/help-faq/)

For your own doll, use the instructions for its exact material and finish. Do not assume a painting upgrade means you should apply cosmetics, sealant or cleaning products intended for something else.

How you intend to dress, display and maintain the doll is part of the purchase decision. A finish that appeals to you visually should also fit the care routine you are prepared to follow.

## Use approval photos for the right purpose

For a custom order with an approval stage, compare the visible finish with the selected configuration and the factory's reference, allowing for the different photographic conditions. The [factory-photo archive](/factory-photos) explains what those pictures can help you review.

Approval photographs can help identify an obvious mismatch. They are not a guarantee of exact screen-to-product colour, a material test or a promise of how a finish will age.

If a detail matters to your choice, keep a reference to it with the selected option rather than relying on a vague description such as "more realistic." That gives the finished build a clearer specification.

## Choose detail deliberately

The useful question is whether you prefer the supported finish on the body you are buying, at its configured price. It is not whether the upgrade has the most elaborate name.
