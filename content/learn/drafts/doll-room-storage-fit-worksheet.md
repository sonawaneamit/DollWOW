# Will the Doll and Its Storage Fit? A Measured Room Worksheet

A storage space can be long enough for a doll and still be awkward to use. The door may narrow the opening, the lid may need more clearance, or there may be nowhere comfortable to stand while moving the doll.

Measure three spaces before buying: the delivery route, the storage interior and the area where you will handle the doll. This worksheet focuses on physical fit. Our [storage guide](/learn/sex-doll-storage) covers choosing an appropriate storage method and protecting the material.

## Gather the right product dimensions

You need more than height. Ask for shoulder width, body depth in the intended supported position, net weight, packed dimensions and packed weight. If using a separate case, obtain its internal and external dimensions and the weight of the empty case.

Here is a real specification reference, checked on 16 October 2026. FunWest's [Bea FWD041 factory page](https://funwestdoll.com/product/fwd041-006-european-bea/) lists a 162 cm doll, 37 kg net weight, 44.5 kg gross weight and a 150 × 39 × 29 cm package. The shorter package length does not establish the doll's suitable long-term storage position.

The [DollWOW Bea listing](/products/funwest-dolls-bea-162cm-f-cup-tpe-companion-doll-fwd041) lists a 30 cm shoulder width. That is not the maximum width of a padded storage arrangement. Arms, supports and the selected position can require more room.

## Walk the route before measuring the cupboard

Start where the carrier can actually leave the package. Follow the route through entrances, corridors, stairs and the final room. Measure clear openings with doors in their usable position, not the outside of the frame.

| Location | Measurement to write down |
| --- | --- |
| Narrowest door | Clear width and height |
| Tightest corner | Approach width, turning space and obstacles |
| Stairs | Usable width, landings and headroom |
| Storage access | Opening width, height and space in front |
| Storage interior | Usable length, width and depth after padding |
| Handling area | Room for support, lifting assistance and an open lid |

A straight doorway check is not a turning check. A long box may clear the door and then meet a wall before it can rotate. Do not rely on diagonal arithmetic alone for a difficult staircase or landing.

## Work through a hypothetical room

The room measurements below are hypothetical, not measurements of a customer's home or a tested installation.

Suppose a cupboard has a 170 × 55 × 40 cm usable interior, but its door opening is only 45 cm wide. An illustrative case measures 165 × 50 × 35 cm outside and 160 × 45 × 30 cm inside.

The case appears to fit the cupboard interior. It does not pass straight through the 45 cm opening in the orientation assumed, because it is 50 cm wide. A different orientation might be possible, but needs a physical route check rather than a promise.

Its 160 cm internal length is also shorter than Bea's listed 162 cm height. That does not become acceptable merely because the original shipping box is shorter. The manufacturer would need to confirm a suitable supported position; forcing a fit is not a storage plan.

This example catches two different problems: access and internal fit. Changing the cupboard does not automatically fix the case.

## Add weight to the worksheet

For an illustration only, a 12 kg empty case plus a listed 37 kg doll makes 49 kg before additional supports or supplies. That is a loaded-case calculation, not Bea's shipping weight.

Plan who moves each load and where it can be set down. Wheels do not remove the difficulty of steps or lifting into storage. If routine access would require help that is rarely available, consider a different storage arrangement or a more manageable build.

## Make the final check practical

Use an empty cardboard template or marked floor footprint to check access without loading the doll. Allow for padding, handles, opening the case and reaching the supported body. A template checks space, not structural capacity.

Ask about any unresolved body depth, approved position or support requirement before ordering furniture. Do not infer a hanging-load rating from cupboard dimensions.
