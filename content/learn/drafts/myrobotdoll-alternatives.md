## Decide Whether You Actually Need a Robot

Many buyers search for a robot when they mainly want one or two features: warmth, sound, limited mouth or eye movement, a realistic physical companion, easier posing, or conversation through a separate device.

Separate the product into five possible levels:

1. a conventional poseable doll with no powered movement
2. a conventional doll with one supported electronic option
3. conversational software used alongside a doll
4. an electronic or robotic head with documented movement
5. an advanced humanoid platform with motors, sensors, and software

These are not interchangeable products. AI software does not create physical movement without motors and controls. One powered function does not make the complete body autonomous.

## Verify Every Technology Claim Separately

If a product claims voice, memory, vision, touch response, facial movement, heating, or body movement, ask for product-specific evidence.

### Conversation and memory

Confirm whether conversation comes from a mobile app, cloud service, separate speaker, or electronic head. Ask about supported languages, account ownership, subscription, internet requirements, data retention, and what remains if the service ends.

### Facial or mouth movement

Confirm which parts move, how they are powered, how movement is controlled, whether the exact model is demonstrated, and how the head is cleaned without exposing motors or connectors to water.

### Heating

Heating is a separate option, not proof of robotics. Confirm the heated area, power source, controller, product compatibility, and manufacturer instructions. Use the [body heating guide](/learn/body-heating-sex-doll-guide).

### Sensors, microphones, and cameras

Ask where the hardware is located, when it is active, where data is processed, whether there is a physical off control, and how the product is reset or transferred.

If current documentation cannot support the exact function, treat it as unverified rather than included value.

## Ready to Ship Needs an Exact Unit

A ready-to-ship doll should already exist in a confirmed configuration. Verify:

- current warehouse and stock confirmation
- exact body and head
- material and tone

- expected dispatch stage after confirmation
- carrier, customs, signature, and handoff where applicable

Ready to ship removes or shortens the production stage. It does not mean instant delivery, unlimited customization, or guaranteed arrival on a specific date.

## Compare the Physical Doll First

Technology does not correct a poor physical fit. Confirm:

- manufacturer, body, and head
- TPE, silicone, silicone-head, or hybrid construction
- height, measurements, and listed weight in US and metric units
- full doll, torso, or another product form
- handling, cleaning, and storage plan
- head-body pairing and selected options

A conventional doll with one verified feature can be the better purchase when it delivers the interaction you want with less software dependence and a simpler repair path.

## Common Mistakes

### Treating a name as proof of robotics

Require current evidence for each powered movement, sensor, AI function, and control.

### Treating fast delivery as proof of stock

Confirm the exact unit, warehouse, fixed configuration, and dispatch stage.

### Assuming AI moves the body

Conversation software can exist with a completely passive physical doll.

### Choosing technology before the physical product

The body still needs to fit your material, appearance, handling, cleaning, and storage requirements.

### Ignoring software continuity

Apps, subscriptions, operating systems, and cloud services can change. Understand what remains useful without them.
