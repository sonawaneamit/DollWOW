# How to Measure a Doll Head for a Replacement Wig

Measure the head before choosing the wig. Circumference is the starting point, but cap depth and ear-tab position matter too. A label such as "average" is not a universal promise of fit, and the doll's body height does not determine its wig size.

This is a measuring method and worksheet, not a table of measured factory heads. Use your own head's dimensions and the chart for the exact wig you plan to buy.

## Prepare a stable measuring position

Use a soft measuring tape, a clear place to record the readings, and the head's identification from your order. Keep the head supported so you are not trying to hold its full weight while guiding the tape.

Remove a removable wig gently. Do not pull implanted hair out of the way or remove the head unless its instructions permit that procedure. If you intend to use a liner, tell the wig supplier; the intended fitting arrangement should be consistent when you measure and try the cap.

Choose the intended front hairline and nape edge first. On a head without a visible hairline, ask the seller for placement guidance rather than letting the tape drift to a different position on each attempt.

## Take three readings

Wig manufacturer Jon Renau describes circumference, ear-to-ear and front-to-back measurements in its [cap-design guidance](https://jonrenau.com/blog/wig-cap-designs/). Its instructions are written for people; the useful principle for a doll is to use defined landmarks and compare all three dimensions with the chosen cap's specification.

For circumference, follow the intended perimeter from the front hairline, around the nape hairline, and back to the starting point. Keep the tape in contact without compressing the head's surface.

For front to back, follow the centre of the head from the intended front hairline to the nape edge. This helps identify a cap that has enough depth even when the circumference looks suitable.

For ear to ear, follow the wig maker's diagram across the top of the head between the hairline points above the ears. Do not substitute an across-the-forehead reading. Jon Renau places its top-of-head point 7 inches back from the front hairline for its human stock-system method. That fixed landmark has not been validated for every doll head. Use the intended cap maker's instructions and confirm how they apply to your head rather than transferring the distance automatically.

Repeat each reading. If it changes noticeably, recheck the landmarks and tape position before recording a final value. Keep units consistent, or retain both centimetres and inches without rounding away a meaningful difference.

## Send measurements, not just a size label

| Record | Your entry |
| --- | --- |
| Brand and full head code | From the order record |
| Circumference | Measured value and units |
| Front hairline to nape | Measured value and units |
| Ear to ear over the top | Measured value and units |
| Intended wig | Maker, style and cap size |
| Adjustment and liner | Published range and intended liner, if any |

Do not measure a loose wig laid flat and treat that number as its wearable circumference. Ask the maker for the cap's intended fitting range and construction. Adjustment tabs are useful for refinement, but they do not answer every shape mismatch.

## A hypothetical fit check

Imagine your circumference reading falls inside a wig's published range, but your front-to-back reading exceeds the depth shown for that cap. This is a hypothetical example with no model measurements implied.

The correct next step is to send both readings and the exact style to the seller. Ordering by circumference alone may leave you with a cap that will not sit at the intended front and nape positions together.

| Hypothetical finding | What it means for the choice |
| --- | --- |
| Circumference inside range; cap depth too short | Do not buy by circumference alone; ask about a deeper cap |
| All readings inside the chart; ear tabs do not align | Recheck the landmarks and intended cap placement before adjusting tension |
| Chart gives only circumference | Depth and ear-to-ear fit remain unknown; send all three readings |
| Fit appears suitable; fibre heat rating absent | Fit says nothing about heat styling; obtain separate care instructions |

None of these rows supplies a factory head measurement or a tested wig recommendation. They show how to use your readings without turning a partial match into a fit promise.

The same reasoning applies when the cap looks centred but its ear tabs sit unevenly. Recheck the placement and shape rather than tightening one side until it appears secure.

## Try the cap before altering it

Check the wig seller's inspection and return terms before purchase, especially for trimmed lace or worn items. On arrival, follow its fitting instructions and avoid irreversible changes until the fit has been assessed.

Look for a centred front, balanced side placement, a nape that stays where intended, and no need to stretch the cap aggressively. If it slides, ask for advice before adding glue, pins or other fasteners to the doll's surface.

Hair type and styling instructions are separate from cap fit. Do not assume a good fit makes the fibres heat-safe or suitable for colouring.

## Keep the next replacement simple

Save the successful wig's maker, style, size and adjustment position with the head code. Remeasure when changing heads or buying a different cap design.
