# MD Doll Buying Guide: Reading Body Codes, Heads and Inclusions

Start an MD Doll shortlist with the complete body designation, then add the head and finish. Height alone is not enough: the factory uses lettered body versions, and a familiar face can appear on more than one body or in a fixed warehouse configuration.

This guide explains how to read those combinations. The [MD Doll collection](/brands/md-dolls) is the place to browse current products and availability.

## Understand the two codes

MD's factory catalog identifies Bonnie as a 166(B) cm F-cup build with head M-37. It also lists Rose on a 166(C) cm body with head M-38 and Jeane on a 167(A) cm body with head M-40. Those are separate body-and-head identities, not interchangeable names for one doll. [MD's official catalog](https://mddoll.com/)

Read the height and its letter together. Then record the complete head code, including any named construction or function. A seller's display name is useful for remembering a face; the factory codes are more useful when asking a precise configuration question.

If a listing omits the body letter, ask for it before comparing measurements with a factory sheet. Do not fill the gap by choosing the closest-looking photograph.

The stock listing is not a blank custom-order form. Compare its existing configuration with the made-to-order alternatives rather than assuming you can replace permanent features before dispatch.

Mary and Jeane explicitly label their figures as body weight; Bonnie's field says only weight. They are not a confirmed like-for-like weight comparison. DollWOW's head labels omit the factory's hyphen, so retain the original order record when confirming M37/M-37 or M40/M-40 rather than treating punctuation alone as a new head version.

## Separate construction from appearance

Make three short lists for your preferred product: included construction, selected appearance, and separately priced extras. This avoids counting an included feature as an upgrade or assuming that every attractive detail in a gallery is supplied.

For construction, ask for the skeleton, hand and finger arrangement, feet, fillings, and supported head functions. For appearance, record the skin tone, makeup, hair and eyes. For extras, save the actual supported selections and their effect on the total.

The [official MD catalog](https://mddoll.com/) identifies Bonnie as 166(B) with head M-37. That model reference does not establish the included package or price of a DollWOW offer. Use the written specification attached to the product you would order.

## Do not combine three different skin descriptions

Body makeup describes an applied appearance. Skin texture describes surface detail. Bionic skin is a separately named upgrade on DollWOW's Mary and Jeane pages. A dated promotion for that upgrade is not evidence of a permanent inclusion or of the configuration of a warehouse Bonnie. Treat these descriptions as separate fields, not three ways of saying that a doll has the same finish.

Ask which is included on the exact body and which is optional. If the main reason for choosing an upgrade is visual detail, request a reference for that finish on the intended body. A photograph taken under different lighting is a poor basis for estimating softness or durability.

## Make the handling comparison meaningful

Record bust, waist, hips and shoulder width alongside height. Those dimensions help with clothing and storage in a way that cup letters alone cannot.

Keep body-only, complete-doll and packed weights separate. Where a listing simply says "Weight," ask what is included before calculating a difference. A delivery carton weight is useful for receiving the package, but it is not the same measurement as the doll you will move at home.

## Finish with one written build

Choose the body first, confirm the head pairing, and then decide which supported extras matter enough to keep. Save the product link, body and head codes, appearance choices, inclusions and configured total together.

For a warehouse unit, request confirmation of that unit's existing specification. For a custom order, resolve unanswered choices before production.
