# Custom Order Review: A Worked Configuration-Mismatch Worksheet

A useful order review turns "this looks right" into a configuration everyone can identify. The face, body, material, finish and loose accessories should all agree with the written order before production begins.

The example below is a hypothetical worksheet, not a customer case study. It shows how to resolve a mismatch without assuming a particular factory supports an unconfirmed option combination.

## Begin with three separate records

Keep the product specification, your selected options and your appearance reference together. Each answers a different question. The specification identifies the base build, the selections record changes, and the reference shows the look you prefer.

Give the reference a precise description, such as "gallery image three, shoulder-length brown hairstyle." That is easier to discuss than "the main photo," which can change when a product gallery is updated.

If you have several references, decide which one governs each appearance choice. Combining images without that distinction can leave support unsure which hair, makeup or skin tone you intended.

## Work through a hypothetical mismatch

Suppose a buyer chooses a supported body and head pairing. The selected hair field says "wig," but the buyer's note requests "implanted hair like the reference." No brand, price or technical compatibility is assumed here.

| Record | What the worksheet shows | Decision needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product selection | Particular body and head | Preserve exact codes |
| Hair field | Wig | Is this the intended construction? |
| Reference note | Implanted hair requested | Confirm support on this head |
| Price summary | No implanted-hair charge identified | Obtain a revised total if available |
| Production instruction | Two conflicting descriptions | Resolve before authorizing the build |

The mismatch is not proof that the factory has made an error. It is an unresolved instruction. The next step is a question, not a demand to supply an unsupported feature at the existing price.

## Decide what matters most

The buyer now has three possible paths: keep the wig configuration, select implanted hair if the exact head supports it, or consider another supported head if implanted hair is essential.

Those paths may have different prices, appearance choices and timing. Ask for the complete consequence of a change rather than approval of one word in isolation. A new head may affect the connector, skin pairing or another selected feature.

Write the final decision in plain language. For example: "Keep the selected head and the included wig; the implanted-hair note is withdrawn." This is an illustrative instruction, not a report of an actual order outcome.

## Close the mismatch in a revision log

Continue the same invented example with a short acceptance record:

| Version | Recorded instruction | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Hair field: wig; free-text note: implanted hair | Conflicting; not the production instruction |
| B | Keep selected head and included wig; withdraw implanted-hair note | Illustrative buyer decision, awaiting the matching written summary |
| Accepted record | Version B repeated consistently in option summary and reference note | Proceed only after the real order's remaining questions are answered |

This is not a report that DollWOW approved a customer's Version B. It demonstrates what must change in the paperwork: the option field, the note and the accepted summary must say the same thing. No invented response time, production date or successful customer outcome is attached to the example.

## Replace the conflicting record

After a decision, save one accepted specification. It should identify the body and head, materials, appearance selections, installed options, included loose items and agreed price basis.

Ask which version support is using if several summaries exist. A revised email should not leave an older conflicting note looking equally authoritative. Keep previous records for context, but clearly identify the final accepted configuration.

For the broader range of choices, use our [custom-doll guide](/learn/custom-sex-dolls). This worksheet is about resolving contradictions within one build, not choosing every available upgrade.

## Resolve structural questions before production

DollWOW's [ordering process](/how-ordering-works) separates early structural decisions from later visual review. It says unclear or incompatible selections are addressed before production or shipment, and structural changes need to be requested before production begins.

That makes the early review especially important for body, head, skeleton and internal features. Factory photographs may help assess visible appearance where available, but they cannot establish every hidden component or reverse a completed structural choice.

If you notice a mismatch after ordering, contact support promptly with the order reference and the precise conflicting fields. Do not assume there is a guaranteed window in which every change remains possible.

## Finish with an acceptance checklist

Read the accepted build once as a specification and once against the reference image. Confirm that each must-have appears explicitly, each paid change has a price, and every unresolved item has an answer.

Then save the final record somewhere accessible for approval and aftercare. Browse [custom builds](/shop/custom) with this approach in mind: one clear set of instructions is more valuable than a long chain of hopeful assumptions.
