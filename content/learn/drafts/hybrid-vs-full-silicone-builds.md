# Silicone Head With TPE Body or Full Silicone? A Same-Budget Worksheet

A silicone head with a TPE body can make sense when the face you want and the body you prefer are supported together. Full silicone can make sense when the chosen silicone body's construction and finish are your priority. At the same budget, compare complete builds rather than assuming one material label includes more value.

This worksheet is a hypothetical purchasing exercise, not a matched-price test, customer case study or claim that two current products cost the same.

## Keep the head decision separate from the body decision

In this comparison, "hybrid" means a silicone head paired with a TPE body. Confirm that definition on each listing: the label alone does not identify the exact materials or construction.

A head upgrade changes the head. It does not turn the body into silicone or automatically change the body's weight, skeleton, finish or care requirements. Likewise, full silicone does not mean every possible head function or body upgrade is included.

For a real example of separate decisions, SEDOLL's [LSTPE explanation](https://www.sedoll.com/stpe-series-introduces-a-new-custom-option-lstpe-lightweight-upgrade/) describes a lightweight STPE body option and selected compatible silicone heads. The head pairing and lightweight body construction are distinct choices. That example does not establish options or prices on another manufacturer's product.

## Set a ceiling before looking at extras

Write down your maximum total and delivery destination. Include the money needed for any support, storage and approved care supplies you still lack. The remaining amount is your build budget.

Then choose two actual products you would consider owning. They need not have identical styling, but both should meet your practical limits for size and handling. A cheaper candidate outside those limits is not a useful comparison.

Use current configurations in the same currency. Record a date beside the comparison so a later promotion or exchange-rate change does not turn the worksheet into misleading price advice.

## Work a hypothetical $2,800 ceiling

Every dollar amount in this exercise is invented, in USD, solely to demonstrate the calculation. These are not DollWOW quotes, typical market prices, named products or proof that an option is supported. Both illustrative candidates are assumed to have passed the buyer's size and handling checks.

| Budget field | Illustrative hybrid A | Illustrative full-silicone B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overall ceiling | $2,800 | $2,800 |
| Reserved for storage and care | $300 | $300 |
| Available for delivered build | $2,500 | $2,500 |
| Base build | $2,050 | $2,250 |
| Chosen options | $150 | $0 |
| Delivery allowance | $120 | $120 |
| Tax allowance | $100 | $180 |
| Delivered-build estimate | $2,420 | $2,550 |
| Headroom after the $300 reserve | $80 | -$50 |

Calculate the available build budget first: $2,800 - $300 = $2,500. For A, $2,050 + $150 + $120 + $100 = $2,420. For B, $2,250 + $0 + $120 + $180 = $2,550. Including the reserve, the overall estimates are $2,720 and $2,850.

A fits the illustrative ceiling; B misses it by $50 even though it has no paid extras to remove. The next step for B is a genuinely lower confirmed offer, another candidate or a revised budget, not deleting the care reserve on paper. Replace every allowance with the applicable quote before using the worksheet for a purchase. An unknown tax or delivery amount remains unknown, not $0.

## Spend the difference where it matters to you

In the exercise, A's $80 headroom is a margin against the estimate, not money already saved or a reason to select another feature. If an essential supported option raises its confirmed total by more than $80, it also fails this ceiling. Record an unavailable must-have as "does not meet requirements," even when the price fits.

If the full-silicone candidate fits the same ceiling with fewer extras, consider whether you prefer that particular body enough to accept the simpler configuration. Compare its photographs and documented construction, not a general promise that silicone must be more realistic or durable.

Do the same with weight. Material alone does not rank handling difficulty. Use the confirmed weight of each selected body and head, including any construction changes.

## Write two care plans, even for one hybrid build

A mixed-material doll needs instructions appropriate to each component and its finish. Record which cleaner, surface treatment and repair method are approved for the head and for the body. Do not assume a product accepted for one material is suitable for the other.

Also check the neck connection and colour pairing. SEDOLL's linked explanation notes that identical tone names can look different across materials and limits its documented matching to a particular pairing. Use exact factory confirmation rather than promising a perfect cross-material match.

Full silicone still needs product-specific care, especially around painted finishes, hair, connectors and any electronics. Our [cleaning guide](/learn/how-to-clean-a-sex-doll) helps plan the routine; the manufacturer's instructions settle the exact products and restrictions.
